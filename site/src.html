Dutch infrastructure procurement screen

A screen of Dutch infrastructure procurement for the problems capital-projects advisers work on. Every 2026 notice in the sample was read, scored against four capability areas and checked against its source.

It ranks where advisory work fits the problem a notice describes and whether there is still time to influence it. It does not show that an authority will buy advice, and contract size plays no part in the score.

Three findings

Sheet 01 · findings
FINDING 1 · WHO IS STILL DECIDING

The open strategy questions sit with regional and network owners, not with Rijkswaterstaat

Rijkswaterstaat buys project-control and contract-management advice through its SROK PPB 2 framework (awarded July 2025), so its open questions mostly reach advisers as call-offs, not tenders.

FINDING 2 · CHANGE IN DELIVERY

Renewing assets that stay in use shows up as contract changes

The official notices give reasons for some: pipe brackets on the Oosterscheldekering replaced after a TNO study; extra work at the Krammer locks for radio-coverage problems possibly linked to a nearby wind farm; repairs to Terbregseplein structures outside the A16 scope; TenneT raising a framework ceiling from €800m to €1.4bn.

FINDING 3 · GRID CONSTRAINTS

On grid capacity, the evidence points to the contractor market

What the notices do show is pressure on the companies building the grid: Stedin's high-voltage route programme (estimated €385m over eight years), TenneT's framework increases and a re-qualification of its cable contractors. Grid expansion reads here as a procurement and commercial problem.

    Method & validation

    Sheet 06 · how the screen was built
    reproducible · 11 Python scripts

    The TenderNed public API was the working source; the TED API was unreachable from the analysis environment, but 154 of 183 procedures carry a TED number, so European notices are covered through TenderNed.

    Scoring

    Investment planning

    Scores high when the scope, phasing or business case is still open, or a later phase is not yet committed.

    Procurement & commercial

    Scores high for strategy consultations, competitive dialogue, bouwteam, lotting choices, frameworks and re-tenders.

    Controls & assurance

    Scores high for multi-discipline work in operating assets, many interfaces, fixed deadlines and multi-contract programmes.

    Asset & lifecycle

    Scores high for renewal, life-extension and maintenance of critical existing assets.

    Each score starts from text rules with sentences that negate a scope removed first. All 183 records were then read and 187 scores adjusted, each with a one-line reason shown in the project dossier.

    Validation against the official notice

    The 18 shortlist lead notices plus 8 drawn at random were compared field by field with the official TenderNed PDF.

    Date differences of one or two days are PDF dispatch date versus TenderNed publication date. One content discrepancy: Stedin's notice states three lots where earlier summaries said two; the PDF is used.

    Limits

    • A sample, not a census: newest-first queries per category concentrate it in May to October.
    • Notice descriptions were paraphrased by a language model during extraction; identifiers and codes were checked against the PDFs.
    • The need for advice is inferred from scope, procedure and stage. It is confirmed only where a notice buys advice directly.
    • Only 4 of 183 descriptions state a value, so size could not be used even if wanted.
    • The Netbeheer Nederland capacity map could not be retrieved, and no notice is located to a site. No grid figures are used.
    • One reviewer applied the rubric; agreement between two raters was not measured.
    • Programme clusters were defined by hand; other groupings would move some ranks.
    • Private and semi-public owners publish only what procurement law requires.
